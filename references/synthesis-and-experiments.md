# Synthesis, experiments, and decisions

Use when substantial material has been gathered, alternatives need comparison, or making and testing could answer an unresolved question.

## Move from material to a defensible finding

Separate what was recorded from what you infer. Group observations by relationships relevant to the inquiry: recurring actions, contrasts, sequences, meanings, material behaviors, or conditions. Check exceptions and competing interpretations. Frequency alone does not establish importance; one contradiction may change the direction.

Keep each consequential finding traceable to its supporting material. Explain scope: these participants, this archive, these examples, this rehearsal, or these conditions. State what the finding changes for the project. Avoid presenting the preferred solution as an insight that the research supposedly discovered.

For a large qualitative dataset, choose an appropriate analytical approach. In Braun and Clarke's reflexive thematic analysis, familiarization, coding, theme development and review, refinement, and writing are recursive and interpretive. Themes are developed through analysis, not simply retrieved as objective facts. Do not label any quick grouping exercise formal reflexive thematic analysis, or add inter-rater reliability as a requirement of that approach.

A lightweight affinity grouping may be enough for a project decision. Name the actual procedure and retain enough extracts to check the inference. Counts describe the analyzed material only; they do not establish population prevalence without an appropriate study design.

Sources: [GOV.UK: Analyse a research session](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/analyse-a-research-session), [Braun and Clarke: Doing Reflexive TA](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/doing-reflexive-ta/), and [IDEO.org: Create Insight Statements](https://www.designkit.org/methods/create-insight-statements.html).

## Translate without disguising the creative leap

Use this relation when it helps make a decision inspectable:

`source or study -> observation -> interpretation -> creative possibility -> probe -> decision`

For each proposed possibility, explain which learning it uses, what is adapted to the user's material, and what uncertainty remains. A leap can be imaginative and provisional; it does not need to pretend it follows inevitably from evidence.

For example, an observed recurring interruption in an interview recording might inspire an interrupted musical phrase or an editing pattern. The source establishes the interruption in that recording. Whether the adaptation expresses the desired experience is a new artistic question.

Respect the user's choice of familiarity, novelty, ambiguity, or accessibility. A conventional direction may fit their purpose. If alternatives converge accidentally, vary the observer, relationship, constraint, interaction, temporal structure, or material behavior rather than changing decoration alone.

## Choose an informative probe

Ask which uncertainty the next artifact can expose. Choose a probe suited to it: a passage for narrative distance, an animatic for timing, an audio sketch for rhythm, a sample for material behavior, a rehearsal for movement, or an interactive slice for player choice. A polished mockup can conceal an unresolved mechanism.

Record the conditions needed to interpret the result: content, version, duration, environment, participant context, or material setup. Identify what would cause revision. Preserve unsuccessful studies when they explain the decision; do not reconstruct a smooth research story after the fact.

For comparison, keep irrelevant differences under control where practical. A finished option and a rough sketch confound the idea with finish. When isolating a variable is useful, hold other conditions steady. When the purpose is open artistic discovery, explore interacting changes and describe them rather than claiming a controlled causal test.

Execute only work within the user's scope. A research-only request can receive a probe design; making an artifact requires authorization implied by the actual task. Physical samples, rehearsals, and participant studies require real execution. If unavailable, deliver an actionable procedure and identify the evidence still needed.

Sources: [IDEO.org: Rapid Prototyping](https://www.designkit.org/methods/rapid-prototyping.html) and [Design Council: Framework for Innovation](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/). These support iterative learning; this skill adapts the process to creative projects without requiring a fixed sequence.

## Interpret feedback at the right level

Choose questions appropriate to the intended encounter. For comprehension, ask what people understood before explaining the concept. For experience, invite descriptions of what they noticed, felt, or expected. For interaction, observe actual choices. Ask about preference when preference is the question; do not treat preference as proof of clarity or effectiveness.

Record the wording and presentation order when they might shape responses. Preserve criticism, disagreement, and unexpected readings. Explain who responded and in which conditions. A speculative artifact may succeed by opening a meaningful disagreement; consensus is not always the goal.

Distinguish:

| Evidence | Appropriate conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| Agent's inspection of an artifact | A craft observation or proposed interpretation |
| Responses from actual participants | What those participants reported or did under those conditions |
| Structured comparison | Differences supported by that comparison and its limitations |
| Properly designed quantitative study | Estimates or effects within the design, measures, sample, and uncertainty |
| Fictional or AI-simulated response | A hypothesis or question for investigation |

For studies requiring specialist statistical or domain expertise, preserve that dependency rather than inventing significance or declaring a winner. For modest project feedback, report useful observations directly without dressing them as a scientific experiment.

## Decide and stop proportionally

Explain the recommended choice, its basis, the tradeoff, and remaining uncertainty. Separate a supported conclusion from a preferred artistic direction. A decision can remain provisional when the cost of revision is low.

Stop gathering when the requested question is answered sufficiently, new relevant sources no longer affect the decision, or the remaining uncertainty requires a probe or access you do not have. Source counts, arbitrary schedules, and a feeling of global completeness are poor substitutes for adequacy.

For an academic contribution, provide enough context, method, documentation, artifact access, and reflection for scrutiny. For ordinary project research, use a concise handoff: findings, implications, unresolved questions, and the next action. Do not require every project to publish an exposition or research database.
